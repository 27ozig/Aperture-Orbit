How to Install on Your Phone
iPhone / iOS:
1. Open this website link in Safari.
2. Tap the Share button (square with the arrow pointing up).
3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen.

Android:
1. Open the link in Chrome.
2. Tap the 3 dots menu in the top right corner.
3. Tap Install app or Add to Home screen.
How to Get .ICS Event Files
You can download free .ics calendar files and load them straight into the app via Import .ICS in Settings:

1. NASA SkyCal: Go to NASA SkyCal, pick your year & events, and download the generated calendar file.
2. International Meteor Organization (IMO): Download meteor shower peak schedules at imo.net.
3. SeaSky Astronomy Calendar: Free sky event feeds at seasky.org.

Once downloaded to your device, tap Import .ICS in Settings to automatically map and populate your feed into monthly drawers.
Shooting on Your Phone
1. Keep it totally still: Put your phone on a tripod or lean it against something sturdy. Holding it in your hands will blur the stars or turn off Astro mode.

2. Camera modes:
  • Pixel: Switch to Night Sight and keep it still—it automatically triggers Astrophotography mode.
  • iPhone: Turn on Night Mode and slide the timer to Max (10s–30s).
  • Samsung: Use Expert RAW or Night Mode.
3. Set a 3-second timer: This stops the camera from shaking when your finger taps the screen.
* How to use the Deck with your phone
Shooting on a DSLR / Mirrorless
1. Focus manually: Switch your lens to MF (Manual Focus). Zoom in on the brightest star on your screen and turn the focus ring until the star looks as tiny and sharp as possible.

2. Aperture (f-stop): Pick your lowest f-number (like f/1.8 or f/2.8) so the lens lets in the most light.

3. Shutter speed: Check the Observer Deck tab to see how many seconds you can shoot before the stars start stretching into lines.
Notes & Deck Tips (*)
• Checking your phone's lens info: If you look at photo details in your gallery and see something like 24mm, just type 24 into the Deck calculator and leave the sensor setting on 1.0x.

• How phones capture stars: Phones don't take one giant 30-second picture. They shoot lots of quick 4-second shots and stack them together automatically so stars stay sharp.
